### I. The Anatomy of a High-Conversion Comp Card

In commercial and editorial booking offices, casting directors review hundreds of digital cards and physical cards every morning. A model's composite card (frequently termed a *Zed card* or *casting card*) represents the single most critical asset in physical and digital representation.

A standard physical comp card measures 5.5 x 8.5 inches (A5 equivalent), printed on 300 to 350 GSM matte silk or uncoated cardstock. However, in our increasingly digital-first ecosystem, the PDF and web-interactive comp card must translate seamlessly onto mobile screens and high-resolution color monitors.

Inspect an exemplary interactive presentation on the [Atharva Sharma Official Comp Card](/comp-card), designed with dual monochrome and color panels, precise millimeter specifications, and direct agency contact conduits.

### II. Front Cover: The Defining Commercial or Editorial Anchor

The front cover consists of exactly **one singular photograph**:
- **Framing**: Clean headshot or chest-up portrait shot with a 85mm or 105mm prime lens to avoid wide-angle facial distortion.
- **Lighting**: Diffused, directional daylight or single beauty dish that highlights bone structure without washing out skin texture.
- **Expression**: Direct, calm, engaging eye contact. Avoid exaggerated grimaces or high-voltage commercial grins unless specifically targeting commercial children/teen boards.
- **Typography**: The model's first and last name set in clean, uppercase grotesque sans-serif (such as Helvetica Neue, Inter, or Futura) or timeless high-contrast serif (Bodoni or Didot). Never clutter the front cover with phone numbers or multi-paragraph bios.

### III. The Back Panel Grid: Versatility & Range

The reverse panel showcases your range across four or five carefully selected images accompanied by standardized metric data:

1. **Full-Length Silhouette**: Demonstrates posture, limb length, and shoulder-to-hip proportions in tailored garments.
2. **Kinetic / Movement Shot**: Proves you can inhabit clothes dynamically—a mid-stride runway capture or fluid editorial motion frame.
3. **High-Contrast Profile or Three-Quarter**: Highlights jawline definition, ear placement, and facial symmetry under Chiaroscuro lighting.
4. **Commercial / Relaxed Lifestyle**: Demonstrates warmth, accessibility, and versatility for international retail campaigns.
5. **Standardized Stats Block**:
   - Height (Imperial & Metric)
   - Chest / Waist / Inseam
   - Shoe Size (US / EU / UK)
   - Hair & Eye Color
   - Mother Agency / Direct Booking Contact

### IV. Common Mistakes That Get Comp Cards Rejected

Casting directors frequently flag recurring errors that immediately undermine a model's professional credibility:

- **Over-Retouched Skin**: Airbrushing that eradicates natural skin pores, moles, or character tells bookers you lack confidence in your real appearance.
- **Inconsistent Hair Lengths**: Submitting images spanning two years where hair transitions from shaved buzzcut to shoulder curls confuses producers scheduling shoot days.
- **Selfies & Social Media Crops**: Casual smartphone snaps with digital noise or distracting mirror reflections should never appear on a formal comp card; reserve raw images for digital casting submissions.
- **Cluttered Fonts**: Using cursive, decorative scripts or vibrant rainbow color palettes detracts from the photography. Let the imagery command the canvas.
