> "Retouching sells fantasies to consumers; casting directors buy reality. A polaroid is not a fashion photograph—it is an architectural blueprint of bone and proportions."
> — *Atharva Sharma // Ref: ATH-25-04*

### I. The False Promise of Retouching

In an era of ubiquitous AI filters, computational portrait modes, and aggressive skin-smoothing algorithms, the value of the unretouched digital has escalated exponentially. When casting directors for runway shows in Milan, Paris, or London evaluate new talent, heavily processed editorial photos are routinely ignored. What they require is raw truth: how does the jawline read in flat daylight? What is the authentic proportion of head to shoulder span? Is the skin texture real?

### II. The Four Non-Negotiable Polaroids

A casting desk digital package consists of four foundational perspectives: 1) Full body front view in fitted neutral attire; 2) Full body 90-degree lateral profile; 3) Waist-up three-quarter portrait; and 4) Direct eye-level close headshot. No flattering high-angle camera tricks, no wide-angle lens distortion. The camera lens must be mounted at clavicle height on an 85mm prime lens to eliminate perspective compression.

### III. The Everyday Setting as Test of Presence

Even when shot informally—such as at a terrace cafe wearing a knit vest and ribbed beanie—authentic presence cuts through casual styling. The test of an editorial model is whether the physical architecture remains unmistakable even in spontaneous, non-studio environments. Natural sunlight exposes authentic hazel eye pigments and genuine facial symmetry that no digital grade can replicate.
